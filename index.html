Currently

Getting more books into low-resource languages

A "low-resource" language is one that computers have barely seen: little text online, few dictionaries, almost no training data. That includes languages spoken by tens of millions of people. The knock-on effect is quiet but heavy. Children learn to read fastest in the language they think in, and for a lot of them there is simply nothing printed in it. AI translation won't fix that alone, but for the first time it makes the job small enough for one person to start.

In progress

Translating with models, editing with people

Running public-domain and openly licensed texts through translation models, then working out where a human has to step in. Idiom, tone, names, anything culturally specific. The model gets you a draft, not a book.

I'm keeping notes on what breaks, because the failure cases are the useful part.

In progress

Publishing so it actually reaches someone

Translation is half the work. The other half is format, licence and distribution: getting a finished text into something a teacher can print, a parent can read on a cheap phone, and anyone can pass on legally.

Open licences by default. A book nobody can copy doesn't multiply.

Thinking about

Leaving the data behind

Every reviewed translation is a small pile of parallel text, exactly the raw material these languages are short of. Done carefully, and with the consent of the people whose language it is, the by-product may matter as much as the books.

How I work

A few things I keep coming back to

Start smaller than feels serious

The projects I've finished all started as something slightly embarrassing: one script, one page, one chapter. The ambitious version was always available later.

Share the middle, not just the end

Polished write-ups teach less than "here's what I tried on Tuesday and why it didn't work." The middle is where someone else can join in.

Ask who it's actually for

If I can't name a real person who'd be glad the thing exists, it's a hobby. Which is fine, but worth being honest about before I spend three months on it.

Pointers

People doing this properly

I'm a newcomer to this field, not an authority in it. If the problem above interests you, these are better starting points than anything I've written. Mostly communities that have been at it for years.

  • Masakhane A grassroots research community building NLP for African languages, in the open. masakhane.io ↗
  • GhanaNLP Translation and speech work for Ghanaian languages, including Twi. ghananlp.org ↗
  • No Language Left Behind Open translation models and benchmarks covering 200 languages. A useful baseline to test against. ai.meta.com ↗
  • African Storybook Openly licensed picture books you can translate and reuse. Proof the model works at small scale. africanstorybook.org ↗
  • StoryWeaver A library of children's books built around community translation into hundreds of languages. storyweaver.org.in ↗
  • Common Voice Where you go to donate your voice, if the language you speak isn't in the data yet. commonvoice.mozilla.org ↗
  • Project Gutenberg Seventy-thousand-odd public domain books. The obvious place to find source texts nobody has to licence. gutenberg.org ↗
